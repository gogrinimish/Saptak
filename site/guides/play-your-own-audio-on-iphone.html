Saptak listening guide · Updated October 27, 2026

Play your own audio on iPhone.

You can keep audio files in folders on your iPhone or in iCloud Drive and use a player that opens those folders. Saptak is being built for that approach: select a folder, keep its hierarchy, and play each folder as a playlist without a permanent library import.

Availability: Saptak is currently being tested through TestFlight. The steps below describe the beta; there is no public download or signup link yet.

What you need

  • An iPhone running iOS 16 or later, and access to the Saptak beta.
  • Audio files you own or have permission to use, in a folder available through Files.
  • Enough device storage and a network connection if cloud files need to download.

Start with a small folder

  1. Arrange your audio in Files. For example, create an Audio folder with Music, Podcast Recordings and Voice Notes inside it. Choose names that will make sense when you browse or search later.
  2. Open Saptak and choose the folder. Use the native picker to select your Audio folder. Files locations can include your device and iCloud Drive; access through other providers depends on the provider.
  3. Choose a playlist by choosing a folder. Open a subfolder for a smaller collection, or use Play all in a parent folder to include its descendants. Selecting a track starts that displayed playlist at the track.
  4. Use the player as you listen. Seek, shuffle, repeat or inspect Up Next. Saptak saves the last queue and playback position locally so you can reopen and press Play to continue when the folder is available.
  5. Refresh after changing files. Add or remove files in Files as usual, then refresh Saptak's browser. Start the playlist again to use its updated contents; refresh does not replace the queue already playing.

Will iCloud audio take up space on my phone?

It can. iCloud Drive and other document providers may download or cache files before playback. These are operating-system-managed downloads, not permanent imports into a Saptak library. Providers can also evict downloaded files, so do not assume a cloud folder will always be available offline.

Which audio files can I try?

Saptak recognizes M4A, MP3, AAC, WAV, AIF/AIFF, CAF, FLAC and OPUS filename extensions. Recognition does not guarantee playback: the codec, container, operating system and file availability matter. Start with a local M4A or MP3 file when checking a new folder.

What if a file is missing or will not play?

Check that it opens in Files, that the provider is signed in, and that any required download has finished. Refresh Saptak after moving files. If access has expired, choose the folder again. Do not rename an unsupported file's extension to try to convert it; that does not change its format.

What this approach does—and does not—do

Your original files stay in their folders and are opened read-only. Saptak does not run a synchronization service, supply a music catalog, subscribe to podcast feeds, or export protected streaming-service downloads. Original-file sharing is an explicit action and can create a copy at the destination.
